---
id: rule_judge_recovery_basis_must_be_visible
name: Recovery basis must be trail-visible
---
When recovering from a failed invocation before emitting Friction or recording no-finding, describe the recovery basis only from evidence visible in the inspected target enactment's recorded step sequence. This visibility requirement applies to every final outcome field that characterizes recovery or failure basis, not only to a field literally named `recovery_basis`: do not place a non-visible failed call, inactive-practice error, schema/discovery read, or Judge-side correction basis inside `observation_data`, `discovery_window_basis`, `direct_target_read_basis`, notes, or summaries as though it were target-visible evidence. If the visible target basis is an error result, name that error result as the basis. An `unexpected keyword argument` error identifies the rejected argument name only; it does not by itself expose the accepted material shape, corrected key, or projected schema. Before retrying a final outcome with a corrected argument shape, make the correction basis visible either through a projected schema/material-shape read in the Judge enactment or in the final outcome's separate Judge-invocation recovery account; do not treat the rejected-key error alone as evidence for the accepted key. The corrected final outcome must also preserve the recovery basis structurally: in the emitted Friction's observation_data or the no-finding result, include a recovery_basis field that names the failed step id, the failed material/error result, the corrected step or corrected argument shape, and why that correction did or did not affect the final judgement. When the failed invocation is the Judge's own final-outcome call, such as `judge_emit_friction()` rejecting `enactment_id` before a successful retry with `target_enactment_id`, the successful emitted Friction or no-finding must carry a separate `judge_invocation_recovery` basis naming the failed step/error, the corrected argument shape, the projected schema/material-shape evidence or outcome statement that made the corrected shape visible, and whether that correction changed only the Judge's invocation or also changed the inspected target/judgement basis. Do not leave the recovery basis only implicit in the corrected call. Do not claim that a schema read, projection read, discovery read, or other inspection showed the corrected argument or material shape unless that read is itself one of the inspected target enactment's visible steps before the corrected outcome, or the outcome records the exact visible target step that exposed it. A Judge-side `discover_affordances`, projection/schema read, material-shape inspection, or earlier failed invocation performed during the Judge's own recovery may explain how the Judge corrected its own next invocation, but it is not a target-visible recovery basis and must not be recorded in the emitted Friction or no-finding outcome as evidence about the inspected target unless it is present among the inspected target enactment's visible steps. If the Judge uses such a Judge-side recovery action, keep the outcome's recovery_basis split: target_basis names only the failed target-visible step/error and any target-visible correction evidence, while judge_invocation_recovery names the Judge-side schema/discovery/action separately and states that it is not evidence about the target's conduct. If the corrected outcome carries structured evidence such as `observation_data.recovery_basis`, keep that field to the same target-visible basis: do not put a `schema_read`, projection read, discovery read, material-shape inspection, inactive-practice/list_recent failure, or similarly named recovery-basis component in any target-basis field unless that read or failure is present in the inspected target enactment's visible steps before the corrected outcome. When the final emitted Friction or no-finding describes how a failed emission was corrected, keep that correction account out of `observation_data` fields that purport to describe the inspected target; put it only in a clearly separate Judge-invocation recovery field, and state that it did not supply evidence about the target unless the same step is visible in the target trail. In particular, a claimed `schema_read_after_failure` or `discover_affordances` basis is valid target evidence only when that schema/discovery step is visible in the inspected target enactment; otherwise the target_basis must name the visible failed step and error, such as the TypeError from the failed emit attempt, while any Judge-side schema recovery is separated as judge_invocation_recovery.

When `judge_invocation_recovery` describes the Judge's own failed call or correction path, that recovery account also needs a trail-visible basis in the Judge enactment being recorded. Name only failed invocations, schema/discovery reads, corrected calls, or error results that appear as recorded steps in that Judge enactment before the final outcome, or are made explicit by a projected step/result in that same enactment. If an attempted call happened before `switch_practice`, before an active Judge enactment existed, or otherwise produced no recorded step in the Judge trail, do not include it as `failed_initial_*`, inactive-practice recovery, or similar failure basis in the emitted Friction or no-finding. The outcome may say no recorded Judge-side recovery basis is available, or omit the unrecorded attempt; it must not present an unrecorded pre-switch failure as though it were auditable trail evidence. This amendment addresses Friction 713's `recovery_basis_not_trail_visible` case, where a no-finding cited a failed initial `list_recent_enactments` call before switching practice even though the inspected Judge enactment's returned steps began only with the later successful list call.

When the failed invocation was a final outcome surface such as `judge_emit_friction` or `judge_record_no_finding`, correcting the material's argument shape does not authorize changing the inspected target. Preserve the same controlling target enactment id from the dispatched target, discovery-window comparison, and direct read in the corrected final outcome. If judgement must redirect to a different enactment id surfaced during discovery, record the redirection basis before the direct read/bundle comparison and include that basis in the final outcome; otherwise a corrected emission or no-finding that switches from the failed call's named target to another id is a `trail_identity_gap`, even when the schema correction itself is valid. This amendment addresses Friction 548 by making the missing recovery-basis requirement explicit: the failed `enactment_id` keyword error shows only the rejected key, so a successful retry with `target_enactment_id` must expose the corrected argument-shape basis in a separate Judge-invocation recovery account rather than relying on the error alone.

When a Judge final outcome says a schema read or `discover_affordances` exposed a corrected argument shape after failed `emit_friction` or `no_finding_outcome` calls, the trail must show that read before the corrected final outcome or the outcome must confine it to `judge_invocation_recovery`. Do not put that claimed schema/discovery basis in `observation_data`, `direct_target_read_basis`, `discovery_window_basis`, or any target-evidence field unless the inspected target enactment's visible steps include the schema/discovery read before the corrected outcome; otherwise name only the target-visible failed step and error as target evidence.

Before including a claimed schema/discovery recovery in target evidence, compare it against the actual returned target step ids from `read_enactment_steps`: if no step in that returned sequence is the claimed `discover_affordances`, projection/schema, or material-shape read, the Judge must not write that claim into any target-basis field and must instead keep it in `judge_invocation_recovery` or omit it.

For the specific recovery label `corrected_after_schema_read`, require an actual schema-bearing step in the relevant visible step sequence before using that label. If the target-visible recovery evidence is only an invalid-material error followed by an `unexpected keyword argument` TypeError, the final outcome must not call that a schema-read correction; it must describe the basis as error-guided material/argument recovery, name the failed step ids and error messages, and keep any inferred accepted argument shape out of target-basis fields unless a schema/projection/material-shape read is actually present.

Friction 594 confirms a narrower window requirement for claimed schema recovery: when a recovered Judge final outcome states that `discover_affordances(query='friction')` or another schema read exposed the corrected argument shape between a failed final-outcome call and a successful retry, the inspected step sequence must actually include that schema-bearing step in the failed-to-successful recovery window. If the returned target steps jump from the failed `emit_friction` or `no_finding_outcome` call directly to the corrected final outcome, the Judge must not record `schema_read_after_error`, `corrected_after_schema_read`, or equivalent wording as target-visible recovery evidence. The final outcome may name only the visible failed TypeError or material error as target evidence, and any Judge-side schema read that is not present in that window must be separated under `judge_invocation_recovery` as the Judge's own invocation recovery, not as evidence about the inspected target trail.

Friction 595 confirms the same boundary for successful Friction emissions whose recovery basis says a corrected argument came from `discover_affordances query=friction` or an equivalent schema read: if the inspected target's returned step ids show the failed final-outcome call followed by the successful emission with no intervening schema/discovery step, the Judge must not cite that schema read as target-visible recovery evidence. The outcome must either keep the schema/discovery action in a separate `judge_invocation_recovery` account that explicitly says it is Judge-side invocation recovery, or omit it from the target basis and name only the visible failed step id, material, and error result.

Friction 596 confirms the target-basis visibility rule for `schema_read_after_error` claims inside `observation_data`: when `read_enactment_steps` returns a failed final-outcome step followed immediately by a corrected `emit_friction` or no-finding step, with no intervening schema, projection, discovery, or material-shape read in that returned target sequence, the Judge must not describe the recovery basis as `schema_read_after_error` or list schema-read required/optional fields as target evidence. The target basis must name only the visible failed step id, material, rejected argument or TypeError result, and the corrected step id or corrected argument shape; any knowledge of required fields must be recorded separately as Judge-side invocation recovery, or left out if it was not made visible before the corrected outcome.

Friction 1065 confirms the no-finding variant of the same contract: when `judge_record_no_finding` includes `judge_invocation_recovery.schema_basis`, or otherwise says `discover_affordances(query='outcome')` exposed the no-finding schema, that cited Judge-side discovery/schema step must appear as a recorded step in the same Judge enactment before the no-finding. If a direct read of the Judge enactment's steps lacks the claimed `discover_affordances` row, there is no trail-visible schema basis for that account; the Judge must omit the schema_basis or state that no recorded Judge-side schema-recovery basis is available, while grounding the no-finding only in visible evaluation, listing, bundle, and direct step reads. A substantively well-grounded target judgement does not authorize adding an unrecorded schema/discovery recovery account.
