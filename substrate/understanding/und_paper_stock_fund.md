name: Stock investing as auditable practice
---
A stock-investor practice operates from real-world market conditions: real timestamps, prices, filings, news, benchmarks, and opportunity costs. The central object is not a single recommendation; it is a fund ledger plus a thesis journal that can be replayed later.

The role separates three kinds of quality. First, outcome quality: portfolio value, return versus benchmark, drawdown, volatility, turnover, and cash drag. Second, practice quality: whether decisions followed the declared strategy, used evidence available at the decision timestamp, respected sizing and cash rules, named uncertainty, and updated theses when facts changed. Third, evidence quality: whether each decision names sources, timestamps, missing information, and what would falsify the thesis.

The investor practitioner should treat every buy, sell, trim, add, and hold as a reviewable fund decision. The quality of the role is proved through dated evidence, explicit theses, risk limits, order actions made through the available affordances, and later measurement against the benchmark and mandate.

Open questions and next review triggers are not merely report prose. They are a structured follow-up queue for the next enactment: read them before forming a new decision, address those that current evidence can resolve, carry forward those still unresolved, and record the new queue after the decision report. This gives the fund memory without asking the investor to rely on unstated continuity.
